# Bias audit — 2026-08-04

An adversarial pass over the code that produces every number in this repo:
`backtest/engine.py`, `backtest/walkforward.py`, `execution/sizing.py`,
`features/indicators.py` and all six strategies.

The question asked at each step was not "is this correct?" but **"how would
this flatter a result without anyone noticing?"**

---

## Findings

### 1. Deflated Sharpe counted the wrong N — FIXED

**Severity: high.** It changed reported verdicts.

`trial_sharpes` accumulated one entry per *evaluation*: folds x grid size. For
the SMA crossover that is 17 x 8 = **136 trials, when only 8 distinct
parameter sets exist**. Re-testing the same eight configurations on
overlapping windows is not 136 independent searches.

Two things went wrong as a result:

- `expected_max_sharpe` rises with N, so the bar a result had to clear was set
  far too high, and every strategy was judged more harshly than it deserved.
- The variance term became meaningless: it mixed *spread across parameters*
  with *spread across time periods*, which are unrelated sources of variation.

**Fix.** Sharpes are now tracked per configuration as well as per evaluation,
and both deflated Sharpes are reported: `DSR` (conservative, N = evaluations)
and `DSRc` (lenient, N = distinct configurations).

**Why both, rather than picking the right one:** which N is correct is
genuinely arguable — walk-forward makes a fresh selection each fold, so the
truth sits between the two. This fix makes results look *better*, which is
exactly when quietly adopting the flattering number would be the real error.
Where the two straddle 0.95, the verdict now reads `BORDERLINE` instead of
`REJECT`, and says the conclusion depends on a debatable choice.

### 2. Look-ahead — CLEAN

Checked and found sound:

- `engine.run` hands strategies `df.iloc[:i+1]`, a slice that cannot contain a
  future bar, and fills at bar `i+1`'s open.
- `DonchianBreakout` excludes the current bar from both channels (`[:-1]`).
  Including it would make the breakout rule trivially self-satisfying — the
  classic look-ahead bug wearing a strategy's clothes.
- All indicators are backward-looking: `rolling`, `ewm`, `diff`. No negative
  shifts, no centred windows, no full-series `fit` anywhere.
- Train and test windows never share a bar, and folds never overlap. Both are
  now pinned by tests rather than by inspection.

### 3. Stitched out-of-sample curve — CLEAN

Fold equity segments are stitched as *returns*, not levels. Returns are
scale-invariant, so the join introduces no artificial step. Duplicate index
entries are dropped and the result is verified monotonic.

### 4. Strategy state — CLEAN

`RsiMeanReversion` and `DonchianBreakout` hold a position flag between bars.
The walk-forward factory builds a fresh instance per run, so state never leaks
between folds or between parameter sets. Tested.

### 5. Meta-level selection bias — OPEN, unquantifiable here

The six strategy families and their parameter grids were chosen *today*, by
someone who knows how markets behaved over the test period. The deflated
Sharpe corrects for searching within a grid; it cannot correct for the grid
having been picked with hindsight.

No fix exists in code. The only real mitigation is out-of-sample in the
strongest sense: test on symbols and periods that played no part in choosing
what to test. That is the next loop item.

### 6. `rsi_last` tail truncation — ACCEPTED

The fast path computes RSI over a 140-bar tail rather than all history. The
EWM seed therefore differs from a full-history computation by roughly 5e-5
relative — far below anything that changes a signal. Equality against the
pandas reference is tested on a common series.

### 7. Universe survivorship bias — DOCUMENTED, contained

The tracked list is "most profitable US companies as of 2026", selected with
hindsight. Backtesting it would be meaningless. Validation runs on SPY only;
the warning appears in `universe.py`, the README, the Chinese manual and the
dashboard itself.

### 8. Cash earns 0% — ACCEPTED, conservative

Idle cash accrues nothing unless `risk_free_annual` is set, which *understates*
strategies that sit flat. This biases against the strategies, not for them, so
it cannot manufacture a false positive.

## What this audit did not cover

- Live broker behaviour. `AlpacaPaperBroker` has never been exercised against
  the real API; only `SimulatedBroker` is tested.
- Fill realism beyond fixed slippage — no volume or liquidity modelling.
- Corporate actions beyond what `auto_adjust=True` handles upstream.

## Effect of the fix on reported verdicts

Re-running SPY with both trial counts:

| strategy | OOS ret | sharpe | maxDD | DSR (evals) | DSRc (configs) | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SMA crossover | 114.2% | 0.74 | −21.7% | 0.21 | 0.95 | BORDERLINE |
| Time-series momentum | 54.5% | 0.44 | −30.2% | 0.16 | 0.87 | REJECT |
| Vol-targeted momentum | 72.1% | 0.66 | −16.3% | 0.49 | 0.95 | REJECT |
| RSI mean reversion | 11.5% | 0.16 | −28.6% | 0.03 | 0.54 | REJECT |
| Donchian breakout | 119.0% | 0.95 | −9.1% | 0.53 | 0.97 | BORDERLINE |
| **Buy & hold** | **216.4%** | **0.80** | **−33.7%** | — | — | benchmark |

Two moved from REJECT to BORDERLINE. **Neither changes the conclusion that
matters:** both still return roughly half of buy & hold. Whether their edge is
real is now arguable; whether it beats doing nothing is not.

The one property that survives scrutiny is Donchian's **risk profile** —
Sharpe 0.95 against the benchmark's 0.80, with a drawdown of −9.1% against
−33.7%. That is a genuine difference, and it is also exactly what you would
get by simply holding less of the market, for free.

A follow-on presentation bug was found and fixed while reading this table: at
two decimal places, 0.9497 and 0.9503 both print as `0.95`, so the table
appeared to contradict its own verdict. Now printed to three.

## The unseen-symbol test — BORDERLINE resolves to REJECT

`scripts/research_universe.py` re-ran both borderline strategies on the ten
tracked companies. Those grids were chosen while looking only at SPY, so these
symbols are out of sample at the level the deflated Sharpe cannot reach. Each
is scored against buy & hold **on the same symbol**, so the universe's
survivorship bias cancels instead of flattering.

| | beat buy & hold on return | on Sharpe | shallower drawdown |
|---|---|---|---|
| SMA crossover | **1 / 10** | 1 / 10 | 7 / 10 |
| Donchian breakout | **0 / 10** | 0 / 10 | 9 / 10 |

**Donchian's SPY result does not replicate anywhere.** Its headline numbers
there — Sharpe 0.95 against the benchmark's 0.80, drawdown −9.1% against
−33.7% — were the best in the whole study. Across ten unseen symbols it beat
the benchmark zero times on either measure.

SMA crossover's one win is NVDA (4803% against 3936%), the most violently
trending name in the universe. One win from ten, on the single most
trend-friendly symbol available, is what chance looks like.

**Both are therefore rejected**, and on much stronger grounds than before: not
"this might be selection luck" but "this was measured on data that played no
part in its design, and it lost".

### The drawdown result is the instructive one

Both strategies produced shallower drawdowns on most symbols while losing
badly on return — 7/10 and 9/10. This is not an edge. It is what happens when
a strategy sits in cash part of the time: less exposure, less pain, less
money. The same profile is available for free by simply buying less of the
index, with no rules, no fees and no maintenance.

This was written into `research_universe.py` as a prediction before the run.
It came true on both strategies. Anything that looks like risk-adjusted
outperformance should be checked against "am I just holding less?" first.

## Verdict

One high-severity finding in the harness, fixed. No look-ahead anywhere. And
the two strategies the fix promoted to BORDERLINE both failed the stronger
test that followed.

The pipeline is sound. The strategies are not. That is the system working —
it took an idea that looked like the best result in the study and killed it
with evidence, in an afternoon, for free.
